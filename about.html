Professional biography

Biography

I am a PhD Research Scholar at the Centre for Artificial Intelligence and Robotics (CAIR), Indian Institute of Technology Mandi, where I have pursued doctoral research in robotics and artificial intelligence since February 2022. My work develops vision-driven control and learning methods that allow single- and dual-arm manipulators to operate in real time, coordinate safely, and transfer reliably from simulation to hardware.

My doctoral research centres on real-time, vision-based collaborative manipulation. I design kinematics-based motion planning and dynamics-based control for dual-arm manipulators, and increasingly fold reinforcement learning into these systems so that bi-manual robots can allocate tasks, avoid one another, and track demanding trajectories without hand-tuned heuristics. I have also worked on kinematic biped locomotion, and I validate my methods at scale in high-fidelity simulation using NVIDIA Isaac Sim and Isaac Lab before transferring them to physical platforms such as the Kinova Gen3 Lite.

Before joining IIT Mandi, I served as an Assistant Professor of Mechanical Engineering at Gayatri Vidya Parishad College of Engineering (GVPCE), Visakhapatnam, where I supervised final-year projects and carried department-level academic and administrative responsibilities. I hold an M.Tech in Computer Integrated Design and Manufacturing from NIT Jamshedpur and a B.Tech in Mechanical Engineering from IIIT R.K. Valley.

Academic journey

My training has moved deliberately from mechanical engineering toward computational robotics. An undergraduate foundation in mechanical engineering gave me a grounding in mechanics and design; a master's in computer-integrated design and manufacturing extended that into simulation, modelling, and automation; and my doctoral work at CAIR, IIT Mandi has concentrated on the perception, planning, learning, and control that turn a manipulator into an autonomous system.

Research vision

I believe robotics advances most when perception, planning, and control are designed together rather than in isolation. I favour methods that are validated rigorously in simulation and then demonstrated on real hardware, because sim-to-real transfer is where many promising ideas quietly fail. I value real-time performance, reproducibility, and safety guarantees, and I prefer hybrid approaches that retain the structure and interpretability of classical robotics while drawing on learning where it genuinely helps.

Current research

My current projects develop real-time collaborative dual-arm manipulation. They include an improved artificial-potential-field framework for asymmetric dual-arm systems; competitive task allocation with real-time, vision-based collision avoidance on two Kinova Gen3 Lite arms; continuous trajectory tracking for non-spherical-wrist manipulators through a learned differential inverse-kinematics policy trained in Isaac Lab and transferred zero-shot to hardware; and adaptive policy blending in multi-agent reinforcement learning for cooperative dual-arm alignment. Together these efforts pursue manipulators that perceive their surroundings, coordinate with one another, and act safely at interactive rates.

Future directions

Looking ahead, I intend to extend my work toward embodied AI for general-purpose manipulation: learning-based controllers that scale across tasks and platforms, tighter integration of perception and control for contact-rich manipulation, and multi-arm and multi-robot coordination with provable safety. I am especially interested in methods that combine the guarantees of model-based robotics with the adaptability of reinforcement learning, and in pushing reliable sim-to-real transfer from single skills toward whole task repertoires.

Research impact

My research has produced peer-reviewed contributions at established robotics and automation venues — including IEEE CASE, ICCMA, and ICCAS — and a journal article in Frontiers in Robotics and AI, with further work under review at Results in Engineering, the Journal of Intelligent & Robotic Systems, and Array, and a paper accepted at the Indian Control Conference 2026. The methods target industrial manipulation problems such as automated bolt sorting and handling, safe multi-arm operation, and cooperative dual-arm tasks, and they are demonstrated both in high-fidelity simulation and on real hardware. A fuller account appears on the Research and Publications pages.
