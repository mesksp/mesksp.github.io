Research

My research is organised around three connected themes: single-arm vision- and learning-based manipulation, perception-based dual-arm motion planning, and reinforcement-learning-based manipulation. Each unites perception, planning, learning, and control toward dexterous, deployable robots.

Theme 01

Single-Arm Vision- and Learning-Based Manipulation

A single manipulator is the building block of every larger system, so this theme makes one arm perceive and act reliably on real hardware. It covers two complementary routes: vision-based pipelines that locate, size, and handle industrial parts, and learning-based controllers that track end-effector trajectories on arms whose geometry defeats closed-form kinematics.

Research challenges

  • Detecting and localising small, similar-looking industrial parts such as new and aged bolts under varying conditions.
  • Turning perception output into robust pick-and-place on a real manipulator.
  • Controlling arms with non-spherical wrists, where closed-form inverse kinematics does not apply.

Current work

For vision, I built an automated pipeline that identifies, sizes, localises, and sorts bolts with a manipulator; DeepLabV3+ gave the best segmentation, with a mean IoU of 0.95 on a custom dataset (ICCMA 2024 and CASE 2024). For learning, I trained a differential inverse-kinematics policy with PPO in Isaac Lab that tracks continuous trajectories on a non-spherical-wrist manipulator and transfers zero-shot to a Kinova Gen3 Lite at 20 Hz (under review at Results in Engineering).

Future directions

I aim to broaden perception from known industrial parts to more general object sets, and to combine vision and learned control so that a single arm can grasp and track from visual feedback end to end.

Theme 02

Perception-Based Dual-Arm Motion Planning

Two arms can accomplish what one cannot, but sharing a workspace demands coordination, task allocation, and real-time collision avoidance driven by what the arms see. This theme develops perception-driven planning and control for dual-arm systems, demonstrated on two Kinova Gen3 Lite manipulators.

Research challenges

  • Avoiding inter-arm and arm-object collisions in real time as both arms move through a shared workspace.
  • Allocating tasks between arms when goals compete for the same objects or regions.
  • Sharing perception between arms so that either can detect a target and the better-placed arm acts.

Current work

Dual-arm cooperative manipulation through shared vision uses eye-in-hand ArUco detection and a reachability-based rule to choose the acting arm (accepted at the Indian Control Conference 2026). Kinematics-based distance monitoring gives real-time safety margins (ICCAS 2025). The improved artificial-potential-field framework avoids inter-arm collisions for asymmetric arms (Frontiers in Robotics and AI 2025), and competitive task allocation with temporal proposal windows builds on it with vision-fed, real-time collision avoidance (under review at the Journal of Intelligent & Robotic Systems).

Future directions

I plan to scale coordination beyond two arms, tighten the loop between perception and planning, and carry empirical safety guarantees from simulation onto hardware.

Theme 03

Reinforcement-Learning-Based Manipulation

Hand-engineered controllers struggle to cover the variety of poses and tasks real manipulation presents. This theme trains policies at scale in simulation with reinforcement learning, then transfers them to hardware, from single-arm control to cooperative dual-arm alignment.

Research challenges

  • Designing rewards and observations that give stable, sample-efficient training.
  • Achieving zero-shot sim-to-real transfer without per-task retuning.
  • Coordinating two learning arms while keeping the structure and interpretability of a model-based prior.

Current work

A PPO policy trained in Isaac Lab over roughly 1.5 million reachable poses maps pose error to joint velocities for continuous trajectory tracking and transfers zero-shot to hardware (under review at Results in Engineering). Gated Prior Composition (GPC) freezes a single-arm reaching policy and trains a MAPPO coordination policy against it under centralised training with decentralised execution, blending the two through a per-arm gate; it reaches 95.9% reach and 93.9% alignment success in simulation and 90.0% alignment over 30 hardware trials (under review at Array).

Future directions

I intend to deepen the integration of RL with classical control, pursue residual and hierarchical schemes that preserve safety and interpretability, and extend multi-agent coordination to larger teams of arms.
