Research

My research is organised around seven connected themes, from vision-based manipulation to embodied AI. Each unites perception, planning, learning, and control toward dexterous, deployable robots.

Theme 01

Vision-Based Robotic Manipulation

Reliable manipulation begins with reliable perception. This theme builds real-time pipelines that turn RGB-D observations into the object poses, geometry, and scene understanding a manipulator needs to grasp, place, and sort with precision. My work has applied vision-based perception to industrial tasks such as automated bolt sorting and handling, and to pose estimation that feeds directly into collision-aware planning for cooperating arms.

Research challenges

  • Estimating accurate object pose and orientation from RGB-D data fast enough for closed-loop control.
  • Maintaining robustness under clutter, occlusion, and varying lighting on real hardware.
  • Coupling perception tightly with planning and control so that vision drives action at interactive rates.

Current work

I use oriented-bounding-box detection (YOLOv8-OBB) running at around 30 FPS to estimate object pose, and feed these estimates into a real-time control loop for dual-arm sorting and handling. Vision-derived pose also seeds the line-segment distance computations that keep cooperating arms clear of one another and of the workpieces they manipulate.

Future directions

I aim to broaden perception from known industrial parts toward more general object sets, integrate learned representations for contact-rich and deformable-object manipulation, and tighten the perception-to-control loop so that visual feedback corrects motion continuously rather than only at planning time.

Theme 02

Dual-Arm Manipulation

Two arms can accomplish what one cannot, but coordination introduces hard problems in task allocation, timing, and collision avoidance. This theme develops planning and control for asymmetric and collaborative dual-arm systems that share a workspace safely and productively, demonstrated on dual Kinova Gen3 Lite manipulators.

Research challenges

  • Avoiding inter-arm and arm-object collisions in real time as both arms move through a shared workspace.
  • Allocating tasks between arms efficiently when goals compete for the same objects or regions.
  • Coordinating asymmetric arms whose reachable workspaces and dynamics differ.

Current work

I developed an improved artificial-potential-field (iAPF) framework for asymmetric dual-arm manipulation that uses a line-segment distance matrix and exponential attraction with velocity damping for smooth, collision-aware motion. Building on this, a competitive task-allocation scheme with real-time collision avoidance runs a roughly 10 Hz control loop fed by 30 FPS vision, and a kinematics-based distance-monitoring method provides real-time safety margins for multi-arm operation.

Future directions

I plan to scale coordination beyond two arms, incorporate learning-based policies for cooperative tasks such as alignment and assembly, and develop coordination methods that carry formal or empirical safety guarantees from simulation onto hardware.

Theme 03

Robot Learning

Hand-engineered controllers struggle to scale across the variety of poses, tasks, and contacts that real manipulation presents. This theme trains policies — at scale in simulation — that capture manipulation skills directly and transfer them to hardware, reducing the reliance on hand-tuned heuristics.

Research challenges

  • Learning controllers that generalise across large, continuous regions of the workspace.
  • Achieving zero-shot sim-to-real transfer without per-task retuning.
  • Designing observation and action representations that make learning efficient and stable.

Current work

I trained a learned differential inverse-kinematics policy with PPO in Isaac Lab over roughly 1.5 million reachable poses, mapping a 25-dimensional pose-error representation to joint velocities for continuous trajectory tracking on a non-spherical-wrist manipulator. The policy transfers zero-shot to a real Kinova Gen3 Lite operating at 20 Hz, demonstrating that large-scale simulated training can yield controllers that work directly on hardware.

Future directions

I want to extend learned controllers from single skills toward repertoires of manipulation behaviours, study how learned and model-based components should be composed, and improve the data efficiency and reliability of transfer so that learning scales to richer, contact-heavy tasks.

Theme 04

Reinforcement Learning

Reinforcement learning lets robots discover control strategies that are difficult to specify by hand. This theme applies single- and multi-agent RL to manipulation, with attention to stable training, sensible reward and observation design, and policies that remain reliable when deployed.

Research challenges

  • Designing rewards and observations that yield stable, sample-efficient training.
  • Coordinating multiple learning agents (multi-arm) without destabilising one another.
  • Retaining the benefits of model-based structure while adding learned adaptability.

Current work

Beyond the PPO-based differential-IK controller, I am developing adaptive policy blending in multi-agent reinforcement learning for cooperative dual-arm alignment. The approach is hierarchical and residual: a pretrained PPO base policy is combined with a MAPPO residual under centralised training with decentralised execution (CTDE), with a learned blending coefficient governing their combination, a 92-dimensional global critic, and 47-dimensional local observations.

Future directions

I intend to deepen the integration of RL with classical control, pursue residual and hierarchical schemes that preserve safety and interpretability, and broaden multi-agent coordination to larger teams of arms and robots.

Theme 05

Motion Planning

Manipulators must move smoothly, efficiently, and safely through cluttered, shared workspaces. This theme develops kinematics-based motion planning for dual-arm manipulators, with collision avoidance and distance monitoring designed to run in real time.

Research challenges

  • Computing collision-free motion for multiple arms quickly enough for closed-loop use.
  • Representing distances between articulated bodies accurately yet cheaply.
  • Producing trajectories that are smooth and dynamically reasonable, not merely feasible.

Current work

My improved APF framework plans collision-aware dual-arm motion using a line-segment distance matrix together with exponential attraction and velocity damping, and a complementary kinematics-based method monitors inter-arm distances in real time for safe multi-arm operation. These planners are paired with vision-based pose estimation so that planning responds to the actual scene.

Future directions

I plan to combine planning with learned policies for faster, more general motion generation, extend real-time planning to more than two arms, and strengthen the safety guarantees that planning provides during contact-rich and dynamic tasks.

Theme 06

Embodied AI

Embodied AI asks how perception, learning, and action come together in a physical agent acting in the real world. This theme frames my manipulation research within that broader goal: agents that sense their environment, decide, and act, with skills learned at scale in simulation and carried onto hardware.

Research challenges

  • Integrating perception, learning, and control into a single embodied loop rather than separate stages.
  • Transferring simulated competence to physical robots without degradation.
  • Building agents whose skills generalise across tasks and embodiments.

Current work

My current contribution toward embodied agents is the large-scale training of manipulation policies in NVIDIA Isaac Sim and Isaac Lab and their zero-shot transfer to real manipulators — for example the differential-IK controller trained over roughly 1.5 million poses and deployed on a Kinova Gen3 Lite. This establishes a practical pathway from simulated experience to embodied real-world skill.

Future directions

I aim to move from individual transferred skills toward more general embodied manipulation: agents that combine perception and control for contact-rich tasks, adapt across platforms, and acquire broad repertoires of behaviour through learning in simulation.

Theme 07

Industrial Robotics

Much of my research is motivated by real industrial needs — sorting, handling, safe multi-arm operation, and cooperative assembly. This theme keeps the work grounded in tasks that matter on the factory floor, where speed, reliability, and safety are non-negotiable.

Research challenges

  • Meeting the throughput and real-time constraints of industrial tasks.
  • Guaranteeing safe operation when multiple arms share a workspace with parts and fixtures.
  • Bridging the gap between laboratory demonstrations and dependable deployment.

Current work

I have applied vision-based manipulation to automated bolt sorting and handling, developed real-time collision avoidance and distance monitoring for safe multi-arm operation, and built competitive task allocation for two cooperating Kinova Gen3 Lite arms. These efforts target industrial manipulation problems directly and are demonstrated in both simulation and hardware.

Future directions

I plan to extend these methods toward flexible, reconfigurable manipulation cells, cooperative assembly with multiple arms, and learning-based controllers that adapt to new parts and tasks while preserving the safety and reliability industry requires.
